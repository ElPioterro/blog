# How to Transcribe Solos: My Complete Method

Transcribing solos is one of the most valuable skills a musician can develop. It trains your ear, deepens your understanding of music theory, and gives you insight into the musical thinking of the greatest players. Here's my complete method for transcribing solos effectively.

## Step 1: Choose the Right Solo

For beginners, start with:

- Solos at moderate tempos
- Solos with clear, audible notes
- Shorter solos (1-2 choruses)
- Solos in familiar keys

Good starter solos include:

- Miles Davis on "So What"
- Chet Baker on "My Funny Valentine"
- Wynton Kelly on "Freddie Freeloader"

## Step 2: Listen Deeply

Before picking up your instrument or writing anything:

- Listen to the solo 10-20 times
- Focus on different aspects each time (rhythm, contour, phrasing)
- Try to sing along with parts of the solo
- Get the overall shape and story in your head

## Step 3: Use Technology Wisely

Software can be incredibly helpful:

- **Transcribe!** or **Amazing Slow Downer** to slow down audio without changing pitch
- **Audacity** for isolating sections and looping them
- **MuseScore** or **Sibelius** for notation

## Step 4: Find the First Note

The first note is your anchor point. To find it:

- Play the chord that's happening when the solo starts
- Try to match the note on your instrument
- Determine the scale degree (is it the root? 3rd? 5th?)

## Step 5: Work in Small Chunks

Don't try to transcribe the entire solo at once:

- Work in 2-4 bar phrases
- Get the rhythm first, then the pitches
- If you're stuck, try singing the phrase

## Step 6: Write It Down

As you figure out phrases:

- Notate them right away
- Include articulation, dynamics, and special techniques
- Mark chord changes above the staff

## Step 7: Analysis

Once you have the notes on paper:

- Analyze how the notes relate to the chords
- Look for patterns and recurring motifs
- Study the rhythmic concepts
- Note how tension and release are used

## Step 8: Learn to Play It

Transcription isn't complete until you can play it:

- Start slowly, focusing on accuracy
- Gradually build up to tempo
- Pay attention to articulation and phrasing
- Try to capture the feel and emotion

## Step 9: Make It Your Own

The final step is to internalize the language:

- Extract key phrases and practice them in all 12 keys
- Use fragments in your own solos
- Combine ideas from different transcriptions

## My Favorite Tools

The tools I use for every transcription:

- **Audio processing**: Transcribe! software
- **Notation**: MuseScore
- **Reference**: iReal Pro for chord changes
- **Recording**: Voice memos to check my playing against the original
