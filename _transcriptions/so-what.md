## About This Transcription

Miles Davis's opening solo on "So What" from the legendary album "Kind of Blue" (1959) is a masterclass in modal jazz improvisation. This transcription captures Davis's economical yet profound approach to soloing.

## Analysis

### Harmonic Approach

Davis works primarily with the D Dorian mode, creating melody through careful note choice rather than rapid-fire lines. His use of space is as important as the notes themselves.

### Key Phrases

- **Opening statement**: The three-note motif that Davis uses to begin his solo sets the tone for what follows
- **Middle section**: Note the subtle use of the upper extensions of the D minor chord
- **Final phrase**: The way Davis resolves his solo demonstrates his impeccable sense of form

## Practice Tips

1. Start by learning the solo at slow tempo, focusing on sound quality
2. Pay careful attention to Davis's use of space - the rests are as important as the notes
3. Work on achieving Davis's characteristic tone - slightly breathy but focused
4. Experiment with subtle variations in timing to achieve the loose, relaxed feel

## Performance Context

This solo comes after the head and introduces the first improvisation on the album. The rhythm section maintains the modal framework with minimal harmonic movement, creating a spacious canvas for Davis's ideas.
